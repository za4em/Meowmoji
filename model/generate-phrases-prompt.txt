Write English training phrases for an emoji classifier. One phrase is one to four words. The words are lowercase letters only. No punctuation, no digits, no emoji inside the phrase, no hyphens.

You output three sections and nothing else. No introduction and no closing note. Each section starts with its marker line. Inside a section there is no header and no blank line.

=== private-words.tsv ===
One row per emoji, in the list order below. Columns separated by a tab:
emoji	word word word
These are the private words you will use. If the list below already gives private words, copy those. If it does not, choose two or three from the emoji's name and the sound that animal makes. Then add one to three association words. An association is the first thing a person pictures about that animal: a synonym no other emoji uses, a trait, an action, or a situation. Examples: strong for the bear, cute for the cat, dangerous for the lion, poison for the snake. An association word used for one emoji is private and is written on that emoji's row. An association that truly fits two emoji may be shared by those two only, and each of those phrases still contains the name word.

=== train.tsv ===
Columns separated by a tab:
phrase	emoji
Sixteen rows per emoji, in the list order. The emoji is the character, copied exactly.

=== holdout.tsv ===
Same columns as train.tsv. Four rows per emoji, in the same order.

A private word belongs to one emoji. It appears only in that emoji's rows. Every phrase contains at least one private word of its emoji. Each private word appears in at least four of that emoji's sixteen training phrases.

A shared word may appear with many emoji. It never appears alone. The shared words you may use are:

- color: black, white, brown, gray, golden, red, orange, pink, blue, green, yellow
- shade: dark, light, pale
- coat: furry, fluffy, shaggy, sleek, smooth, woolly, scaly, feathery, soft, bald
- size: tiny, small, little, big, large, huge
- age: baby, young, adult, cub
- sex: male, female
- mood: calm, wild, shy, angry
- motion: running, jumping, swimming, flying, hopping, sleeping

At least ten of the sixteen training phrases for an emoji contain a shared word. At least six contain an association word. Use several kinds, not only color: breed is already a private word when the list gives one, such as labrador or poodle. Also use color, shade, coat, sex, age, sound, size, motion, and association. A sound word is private, so it stays on the one emoji whose list includes it. Association phrases look like "strong bear", "cute cat", "dangerous lion", "poison snake".

Spread each shared word across at least eight different emoji in train.tsv, so a color or the word male does not become the name of one animal. "male lion" is fine because lion is private. "male" on its own is not a phrase.

Do not put one emoji's private word into another emoji's phrase. "poodle dog" is wrong if dog belongs to a different emoji.

The four holdout phrases are new combinations. Every word in a holdout phrase already appears in train.tsv. The private word in a holdout phrase already appears in that same emoji's training rows. Write all of train.tsv before holdout.tsv, and fix any holdout word that is missing from train.

Prefer a unique phrase for every row. The same phrase may appear more than once for the same emoji. The same phrase text is never used for two emoji.

If the reply would be cut off, stop at the end of an emoji, on its last row, and make the final line:
RESUME <index>
The index is the next emoji's index from the list.
